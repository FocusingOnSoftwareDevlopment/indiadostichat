Regional Realms

Indian States

To travel through Indian states is to cross cultural borders. Each state possesses its own administrative history, traditional attire, local agriculture, and regional pride, contributing to the country's federal strength.

India changes every few hundred kilometres, yet something familiar still remains.

The Federal Structure of India

India is a federal union comprising 28 states and 8 Union Territories. These states were largely reorganized along linguistic lines in 1956, a decision that helped preserve regional cultures, literatures, and local media. Each state has its own elected government, legislative assembly, and distinct administrative character, allowing for local developmental policies while remaining integrated under the national constitution.

Northern States: Land of Rivers and Valleys

Northern states like Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, and Uttar Pradesh are shaped by the Himalayas and the plains of the Ganges. Punjab is the agricultural heartland, famous for its energetic bhangra dance and golden wheat fields. Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand offer serene mountain retreats, apple orchards, and spiritual routes. Uttar Pradesh, the most populous state, contains historic cities like Agra, Varanasi, and Lucknow, representing a cradle of classical culture.

Western States: Deserts and Commercial Coasts

Rajasthan, Gujarat, and Maharashtra define the west. Rajasthan is a land of colors, sand dunes, and majestic palaces of Rajput kings, where folk music remains alive. Gujarat, with the longest coastline, is known for its entrepreneurial spirit, salt pans, and the energetic Garba dance during Navratri. Maharashtra is an industrial powerhouse, home to Mumbai, rich agricultural belts, and a proud history linked to the warrior king Shivaji.

Southern States: Tech Hubs and Ancient Coasts

Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, and Kerala represent the peninsular south. Karnataka is a hub of technology and sandalwood forests. Tamil Nadu is characterized by Dravidian architecture, Kanchipuram silk, and deep classical heritage. Andhra Pradesh and Telangana are leaders in agriculture, IT, and cinema. Kerala, known as 'God's Own Country,' is famous for its high literacy rates, Ayurvedic medicine, and coconut-fringed backwaters.

Eastern and North-Eastern States: Hills and Forests

West Bengal, Odisha, and Bihar form the east, while the 'Seven Sisters' (Assam, Meghalaya, Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram, Tripura) and Sikkim dominate the north-east. The north-eastern states are characterized by rich tribal heritage, clean nature (like the cleanest village in Asia, Mawlynnong, Meghalaya), and pristine biodiversity, offering a serene contrast to the bustling plains.
